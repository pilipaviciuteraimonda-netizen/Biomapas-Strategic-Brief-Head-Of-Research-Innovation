01 · The Growth Reality

What €200M actually requires.

Before anything else, leadership must accept one correction to how the AI agenda is sometimes framed internally.

Carbyne Equity Partners acquired a majority stake in October 2024, and the stated target of scaling Biomapas into a €200M+ diversified pharma services platform represents a 9× growth ambition from the €22.7M consolidated revenue reported in 2024. At that scale of ambition, the math requires targeted M&A plus organic growth — AI and automation is the operational platform and integration layer within that broader strategy, not the growth engine itself.

AI is positioned as

  • An operational efficiency layer — recovering FTE capacity without headcount growth.
  • A bid-defense differentiator — making Biomapas wins more defensible in competitive RFPs.
  • An acquisition integration layer — the connective tissue that makes acquired companies valuable faster.
  • A way to make Biomapas more defensible as a PE-backed platform — relevant to any future exit or capital event.

This also means one deliberate strategic decision must be made by leadership now — not by default, and not by the Head of R&I alone:

Is Biomapas building toward an AI-augmented specialty CRO, a platform-as-a-service player for sponsor workflow automation, or a strategic acquirer of niche AI capability in its operational geography?

These paths are not mutually exclusive over time — but they require different capital allocation, different hiring profiles, and different M&A criteria right now. The Head of Research & Innovation is the function that executes whichever path is chosen.

02 · Three Non-Negotiable Foundations

Why three foundations come first.

1

Legal & Regulatory Compliance Framework

This is not a technology problem. It is a liability problem that blocks every AI initiative downstream.

The EU AI Act's high-risk classification for healthcare AI takes formal effect in August 2026. The EMA's 2026 GVP update has already made AI-powered PV tools a regulatory expectation, not an option. The revised UK clinical trial regulations (effective April 28, 2026) add mandatory publication and results requirements that make automation economically necessary.

What must be built internally — in this order

  • AI Use Policy for regulated workflows — a documented, approved framework governing what AI can and cannot do in GxP-validated PV and clinical operations workflows, aligned with EU Annex 11 / GAMP 5 CSV requirements.
  • Client pre-approval protocol — Biomapas cannot use published studies or client-sourced data to generate AI outputs without sponsor pre-approval; every AI-generated response or signal in a client workflow must trace back to a documented approval chain.
  • CSV validation package for each AI tool — every AI component added to Veeva Vault Safety or CTMS must be validated before going live; this adds 3–6 months to any deployment timeline and must be budgeted in advance.
  • Human-in-the-loop documentation — GVP 2026 and the FDA/EMA joint Good AI Practice Principles (January 2026) require that a medically qualified person reviews and approves every expedited ICSR before submission; this gate is non-negotiable and must be explicitly preserved in any workflow redesign.
Bottom line AI integration without a compliance plan is not a technical challenge — it is a direct liability and reputational risk in the eyes of pharma clients who are themselves EMA audit subjects.

03 · M&A Logic

The acquisition and lock-in question.

Partnership relationships are replicable — a competitor can sign the same Saama or Veeva contract. An acquisition creates exclusivity, or at minimum a structural head start, that a partnership cannot. The right acquisition target for Biomapas is a company with a CEE-specific data asset or regulatory intelligence layer that would take a competitor 3–5 years to replicate from scratch.

Three acquisition profile types are worth mapping:

  • A CEE regulatory intelligence platform — structured, machine-readable regulatory intelligence for CEE/CIS markets.
  • A CEE feasibility analytics company with a proprietary site network data layer.
  • An AI-native boutique CRO in Eastern Europe — a defensive acquisition that removes a future competitor and absorbs their engineering talent before they reach Series A.

Specific candidates should be identified and shortlisted only after both verticals are process-mapped and genuine capability gaps are confirmed — what fits Biomapas can only be evaluated properly once the internal operational picture is clear.

Why move now Acquiring a small AI-native competitor before it reaches Series A removes a competitive threat, absorbs their engineering talent and process design, and accelerates Biomapas's own AI capability build by 18–24 months. The alternative — watching them grow into a credible mid-market competitor — is a more expensive problem to solve later.
04 · Priorities & Sequencing

The order of operations.

Process mapping alone across two verticals, with the right people in the room.

  1. First — before anything else Compliance framework and DPA audit. These are prerequisites that gate every other initiative. No AI tool goes live, no vendor conversation becomes operational, until the legal and regulatory foundations are in place.
  2. Second — running in parallel once foundations are confirmed Process mapping across both verticals with the operational teams. This is not a one-week exercise. It is the work that determines every subsequent decision — vendor selection, automation priorities, build vs. buy, acquisition targeting, and EU funding applications.
  3. Third — once the process map is validated Tier 1 automation builds: SUSAR multi-jurisdiction routing engine, eTMF completeness scoring, MedDRA auto-coding activation. These require no new budget, no new vendors, and no GDPR negotiation — they run on existing infrastructure and produce the first measurable results.
  4. Fourth — funded from margin recovered in the third step MVR auto-generation and data structuring for the enrollment prediction model. The data structuring work has a long lead time and should begin as early as possible — it cannot be shortened.
  5. In parallel throughout — not after Acquisition market mapping for the three target profiles above. The window on AI-native boutique CRO targets closes the moment one of them raises a Series A. This mapping runs alongside operational work, not sequentially.
05 · The EU Funding Dimension

The strategic agenda does not operate in a vacuum.

What Biomapas Is — Through the EU Lens

Biomapas is a pan-European CRO (Contract Research Organization) founded in 2001 in Vilnius, Lithuania. It provides pharma and biotech clients with Clinical Trial Management (Phase I–IV; oncology is ~23% of portfolio, neurology ~13%, cardiology ~12%), Pharmacovigilance (PV) — post-market drug safety monitoring, Regulatory Affairs — EMA, national Health Authorities, clinical trial authorizations, and Medical Information services.

Biomapas is not a drug developer or a tech company. It is a services enabler operating across Europe, Eastern Europe, and CIS markets. The new CEO Xavier Duburcq brings international pharma services leadership. This positioning matters: Biomapas sits at the intersection of clinical data, regulatory expertise, and trial execution — exactly the three capabilities the EU is investing in through 2030.

Honest starting point — May 2026

No EU funding strategy work has begun at Biomapas. This section covers only realistic, still-open or near-term opportunities, with no filler.

The EU landscape Biomapas must understand

Three structural shifts define the 2026–2030 environment:

1. European Health Data Space (EHDS) — Regulation (EU) 2025/327

In force since March 26, 2025. By March 2029, all EU member states must activate HealthData@EU — the infrastructure for secondary reuse of health data (including clinical trial data) for research. This is the single most important structural opportunity for Biomapas: they already generate and manage the exact type of data EHDS governs. Under EHDS, Biomapas can register as a Data Holder and a certified data processor, turning their existing clinical data operations into a regulated infrastructure role — not just a service.

2. EU Biotech Act (COM/2025/1022 final)

In EU legislative process; target adoption end-2026/early-2027. Creates the STEP (Strategic Technologies for Europe Platform) status for innovative companies — giving access to preferential EU and national financing — and an EIB Health Biotechnology Investment Pilot for blended finance (grants + loans). Designed for scale-up, not just startups. A 25-year CRO expanding into digital/AI services is a natural candidate.

3. Digital Omnibus — Pseudonymisation Reform (Ongoing)

The European Commission has proposed that "sufficiently pseudonymised" health data should no longer be treated as personal data under GDPR. The AI component of the Omnibus is currently delayed (as of May 2026). Germany is leading discussions to exempt certain industries — including medical devices — from parts of the AI Act. If adopted, this dramatically reduces compliance costs for deploying AI in clinical trial workflows, which are adjacent to medical device regulation. Biomapas should track EDPB pseudonymisation guideline drafts, as these directly affect how trial participant data can be processed and commercialized.

09 · AI Communication & Adoption

Quick wins, credible signals, real value.

Why this section exists

Before Biomapas builds AI infrastructure, it must be seen building it — credibly, specifically, and without overstating what is real today. Internal adoption and external communication are not sequential. They are parallel signals that reinforce each other. Getting both right in 2026 is a competitive advantage in itself.

Where Biomapas can publicly signal AI integration — without overstating reality

LinkedIn is the fastest, highest-leverage channel. The target audience — pharma sponsors, CRO procurement leads, biotech BD teams — lives there. The posts that land are specific, not aspirational:

  • "Our PV team is piloting AI-assisted ICSR triage. Here's what we learned in 30 days."
  • "We mapped AI readiness across two verticals. These were the honest findings."
  • "EU AI Act high-risk classification takes effect August 2026. Here's how we're preparing our GxP workflows."

These are true, defensible, and technically credible without a finished product behind them. Recommended cadence: 2–3 company page posts per month, plus 1–2 personal posts from leadership. Cost: time only.

A single "AI at Biomapas" webpage — honest, current-state, published within 4–6 weeks — answers three questions sponsors will ask: What are you using? How are you staying compliant? What does this mean for our data? Absence from this question is itself an impression.

Partnership and tool announcements only when there is real content. A specific, named announcement ("Biomapas has activated [capability] in [workflow] — here is what it means for CEE clients") lands harder with sophisticated pharma audiences than any generic "AI partnership" press release. Credibility is the asset.

Conference abstracts (Q3–Q4 2026 deadlines). A submission to DIA, SCOPE EU, or PharmaLex on "compliance-first AI integration in a mid-market CEE CRO" is distinctive, accepted, and generates sponsor interest before the presentation is delivered.

Internal AI adoption — where to start, department by department

The principle is consistent: begin with tools that save time on work people already find tedious, require no GxP validation to adopt, and produce results in the first week of use.

Before deploying anything, run a one-page survey per department to identify where people spend the most time on repetitive, low-judgment tasks. This creates buy-in, surfaces real pain points, and gives leadership a defensible, data-grounded basis for tool decisions. It also makes the first AI training session relevant rather than generic.

The four tools that cover the majority of daily use cases — Claude, ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Gemini — each have distinct strengths. The goal is not to standardize on one tool company-wide. It is to match the right tool to the right workflow, build genuine habits, and expand from there. Specific tool-to-team mapping should follow the survey results — not precede them.

AI agents for market and regulatory monitoring — a practical stack

The most underused application of AI in a CRO environment in 2026 is continuous monitoring — not of internal operations, but of the external environment that directly affects client decisions and competitive positioning. The following are deployable today, require no GxP validation, and produce immediate value:

  • Perplexity Pro with Spaces: Create a dedicated Biomapas Intelligence Desk — a shared, AI-assisted research environment where regulatory, BD, and clinical teams monitor EMA, FDA, and national HA guidance in near-real-time, track competitor CRO announcements, and surface relevant EU funding calls. Shared Spaces mean no one is conducting the same search independently. This is the closest equivalent to a competitive intelligence function at zero incremental headcount cost.
  • Claude Projects: Persistent context across sessions — upload Biomapas SOPs, regulatory frameworks, and client-specific background so every output is generated in context, not from a blank slate. Particularly valuable for PV and regulatory teams managing multi-market complexity.
  • ChatGPT Team with custom GPTs: Build 2–3 internal GPTs tailored to Biomapas workflows — a PV Query Responder trained on SOP templates, an RFP Assistant loaded with capability statements and past bid language, a Regulatory Monitor surfacing EMA/FDA updates on demand. Built in hours, not weeks.
  • Google Alerts + Gemini: For teams not yet ready for premium tools — a well-configured Alerts stack (competitor names, key regulatory terms, EU funding keywords) fed into a weekly Gemini summary is a functional, zero-cost starting point for intelligence monitoring.

The internal adoption playbook — five steps that make it stick

  1. Department survey (Week 1). Five questions. Ten minutes per person. Real data on where time is lost.
  2. AI literacy baseline (Weeks 2–3). One 90-minute internal session — not a vendor demo — focused entirely on how to write a prompt that works, using real Biomapas work examples. The gap between "I tried it and it gave me rubbish" and "I use it every day" is almost always prompt quality.
  3. One tool, one team, one month (Weeks 3–7). Resist rolling out everything simultaneously. A PV team member saving 45 minutes per narrative draft is a more persuasive internal argument for AI adoption than any external case study.
  4. Monthly AI roundtable. Forty-five minutes, cross-department, peer format — not a reporting exercise. What worked, what didn't, best prompts shared. Prevents isolated wins from staying isolated.
  5. Compliant tool registry. A living shared document listing every AI tool in use, which departments, what data it touches, and whether it has been reviewed against GDPR and the EU AI Act. Starting it now, when the list is short, costs nothing. Reconstructing it under audit pressure costs significantly more.

What this looks like from the outside by December 2026

If Biomapas executes the above through H2 2026, the external profile by year-end — without fabricating anything — is:

  • 12–15 specific, technically credible LinkedIn posts on AI integration in PV and clinical operations.

  • At least one trade media mention tied to a concrete workflow activation or conference abstract.
  • Named participation in one EU consortium conversation.
  • An internal team that has moved past "I tried it once" into habitual, workflow-integrated AI use across at least three departments.

That is not a finished AI strategy. It is a credible, defensible, externally visible proof of direction — and in a market where most mid-market CROs are still at the announcement stage, it is genuinely differentiating.

01 · Executive Framing

Why this analysis now.

The pharmacovigilance outsourcing market is undergoing a structural shift. This is not a trend to monitor — it is already a competitive reality. The EMA's 2026 GVP update has made AI-powered pharmacovigilance tools and automated signal detection systems an expectation, not an option for MAHs. Simultaneously, the EMA–FDA joint Good AI Practice Principles published in January 2026 have moved AI in PV from "interesting pilot" to regulated activity. 03 · Application Map

Where AI and automation can be applied — an honest assessment.

The process mapping reveals a clear tiered structure of automation readiness. This is not a list of aspirations — it is a prioritized investment map.

1
Act now

High readiness, high value, act now.

ICSR data extraction (OCR + NLP)

The single highest-impact, fastest-deployable automation. Incoming reports arrive as unstructured PDFs, emails, phone call transcripts, and web forms across all EU markets. Manually re-keying data from these into Veeva Vault Safety is labor-intensive and error-prone. AI document extraction using OCR and NLP is already embedded in Veeva Vault Safety's native Case Intake Agent. The question for Biomapas is not whether to use this — it is whether it is currently activated and properly configured for all client workflows, including multilingual inputs from key EU markets (Polish, Romanian, French, Dutch, Turkish). If this is not fully deployed, it is the first thing to fix.

MedDRA auto-coding

MedDRA coding is among the most resource-intensive sub-tasks in case processing. An experienced coder spends significant time per case; dictionary version updates create re-coding cycles. AI-assisted auto-coding with mandatory human confirmation is technically mature, with TransCelerate's industry initiative already benchmarking accuracy. Vendors including ArisGlobal NavaX and Oracle Argus AI modules offer this capability today. The critical prerequisite: the active MedDRA version in the safety database must be locked and systematically updated before any AI coding layer is added.

Expectedness and reportability assessment (rule engine + NLP)

Comparing verbatim event terms against the current SmPC/CCDS for expectedness, and then applying multi-market reportability logic (EU 15-day rule, MHRA 15-day rule, TUFAM 15-day rule) is a rule-heavy task that follows explicit decision trees. This is highly suitable for rule-engine automation augmented by NLP for label comparison. For Biomapas operating across multiple EU member states plus UK and Turkey simultaneously, a validated multi-market reportability engine reduces the single largest risk of deadline breach — and a >99% on-time expedited submission rate is the industry benchmark that client contracts are built around.

Literature screening (PubMed/Embase search execution + NLP triage)

Weekly search execution across PubMed and Embase APIs is already technically automatable — both databases offer programmatic access. The high-value AI layer is NLP-based abstract triage: classifying retrieved articles as "clearly irrelevant," "requires full-text review," or "contains potential ICSR." This step currently consumes significant human attention and can be substantially accelerated with a trained NLP classifier.

A note of competitive context is warranted here. Local language literature screening across EU markets — Polish, Romanian, Czech, Slovak, Lithuanian, French, Dutch, Turkish — is not fully automatable with off-the-shelf tools, because many regional journals are not comprehensively indexed in standard APIs. Biomapas's native-language specialist capability in this area is operationally valuable and difficult to replicate at speed. However, the competitive moat should not be overstated: large global CROs such as IQVIA and Parexel maintain local specialist networks across major EU markets and are actively investing in multilingual AI tooling. Notably, ArisGlobal launched NavaX Translation in February 2026 — a certified, pharma-grade multilingual translation layer embedded directly into LifeSphere Safety workflows — signalling that technology-driven multilingual capability is advancing rapidly.

Biomapas's advantage in this area is real but time-limited and geography-specific: it is strongest in CEE markets (Polish, Romanian, Czech, Slovak, Lithuanian) where global CRO local presence is thinner, and weaker as a differentiator in Western European markets. This capability should be preserved, not automated away, and positioned explicitly to clients as a compliance service — particularly for CEE-heavy product portfolios — while recognising that it will require ongoing investment to remain competitive as multilingual AI tooling matures.

PSUR/PBRER data compilation (automated extraction + table generation)

Data lock point extraction from Veeva Vault Safety, case count tables by SOC and seriousness, interval summary statistics — these are structured data extraction tasks that are fully automatable from a validated database. Several vendors now offer report shell pre-population directly from safety database queries. This reduces PSUR preparation time materially and shifts human effort toward the benefit-risk narrative and QPPV review, where it belongs.

Submission compliance tracking (real-time dashboard)

Veeva Vault Safety already has partial dashboard functionality. A fully configured real-time compliance dashboard — tracking every pending ICSR by jurisdiction, deadline, and current processing stage — is a low-cost, high-value operational tool that both reduces internal risk and creates a visible compliance demonstration for client audits. If not fully configured today, this is a quick win.

07 · Compliance Layer

The AI integration compliance layer is not optional.

A point that must be explicit to the Biomapas Leadership: AI integration in PV is not purely a technology decision — it is a regulatory compliance and legal decision. Three non-negotiable requirements apply:

Non-negotiable · 1

CSV validation for every AI component

Any AI tool added to a GxP-validated system (Veeva Vault Safety) must itself be validated under EU Annex 11 / GAMP 5. This means IQ/OQ/PQ protocols, validation master plan updates, and change control documentation. Biomapas must budget for this before any AI tool goes live in a client-facing workflow.

Non-negotiable · 2

Human-in-the-loop for all expedited submissions

Under current GVP (and the 2026 update), a medically qualified person must review and approve every expedited ICSR before submission. No AI tool replaces this step. AI accelerates the upstream process; the human approval gate is non-negotiable and must be preserved in any workflow redesign.

Non-negotiable · 3

DPA amendments for AI data usage

If Biomapas plans to use any ICSR data — even anonymized — to train or fine-tune AI models, this requires explicit consent in updated Data Processing Agreements with every MAH client. This is both a legal requirement and a commercial negotiation point. New client contracts should include AI data usage permissions as a standard clause from 2026 onward.

Executive Summary

The opportunity is real. The constraints are real. The two need to be held together.

This document provides a grounded analysis of the Clinical Research/Operations vertical at Biomapas, examined specifically through the lens of where automation and AI augmentation can create measurable operational and commercial value — and where they cannot yet, and why. It is structured to support an informed leadership decision on whether establishing a Head of Research & Innovation function makes strategic sense, what that function would realistically own, and what the sequencing of work should be.

Single structural reality

Biomapas operates as a Data Processor under GDPR, not a Data Controller. Every clinical data record it touches — eCRF, safety, imaging, PK/PD — is owned by the sponsor. Any AI use case that trains on or processes trial-specific patient data requires explicit DPA amendment with each sponsor individually. This is not a blocker, but it is a sequencing constraint that must be built into every commercial conversation, every vendor evaluation, and every internal build decision.

It is also one of the most important things to communicate clearly to leadership — because AI vendors will not volunteer this complexity.

Part I · Market

What is actually happening in the market (May 2026).

The macro shift is already priced in

The global CRO market was valued at approximately $72.5 billion in 2026 and is projected to reach $154.9 billion by 2033. Within that, the AI-specific clinical operations automation sub-segment is growing at 34.3% per year. By 2025, AI companies captured 55% of all health tech venture funding globally, up from 37% in 2024. Regulatory bodies including EMA and FDA are actively endorsing AI-powered solutions, with over 60% of clinical trial sponsors now exploring AI-based tools for data analysis and protocol design.

This is not speculative. Sponsors are already reweighting contract decisions: CROs that cannot demonstrate data-driven feasibility, automated compliance infrastructure, or AI-augmented delivery are being systematically de-prioritized in RFP scoring.

Revised UK clinical trial regulations effective April 28, 2026 introduce new requirements including mandatory public registration and results publication within 12 months — increasing administrative burden and making automation economically necessary, not optional. The EU Biotech Act and Digital Omnibus simplification proposals are adding further regulatory momentum for technology-enabled trial operations.

Where large CROs are moving

IQVIA deployed over 50 AI agents in production environments in Q3 2025, improving its Clinical Program Productivity Index by 12% in 2024. ICON has integrated generative AI for protocol drafting and regulatory automation. Veeva Systems — the platform that Biomapas almost certainly uses for Safety, CTMS, and eTMF — announced AI agent availability across all Vault applications through 2026: Safety and Quality from April 2026, Clinical Operations and Regulatory from August 2026. Critically, Veeva AI is usage-based, meaning even a mid-size CRO like Biomapas can access production-grade agentic AI without enterprise-scale investment, provided its Vault configuration is current and its processes are clean enough to benefit from automation.

Strategic implication The technology infrastructure to automate significant portions of the Clinical Research/Operations workflow is becoming a licensing decision, not a build decision — but only for organizations whose internal processes and data are sufficiently structured to make use of it. This is where the maturity gap often sits in specialty CROs.

The competitive threat in CEE

Large global CROs are not Biomapas's primary competitive threat in Poland, Romania, Czech Republic, or the Baltics. They are overpriced and too process-heavy for the mid-size biotech sponsors who anchor Biomapas's client base. The actual threat is a new category of AI-native boutique CROs specifically capitalizing on the mid-market gap in Europe. Companies like Lindus Health (UK, AI-native CRO model, Series A raised 2022–2024), and several Eastern European technology-adjacent CRO startups are building with AI as a core operating assumption rather than a retrofit. If Biomapas does not close the capability gap within 18–24 months, the positioning advantage in CEE becomes harder to defend on technology grounds — even if the site network and regulatory expertise remain genuinely superior.

Part II · Process-Level Analysis

Clinical Research / Operations vertical.

The Clinical Research/Operations vertical is operationally the most complex service line Biomapas runs. It spans 12 service sub-lines — from Business Development and bid defense through Clinical Strategy, Study Start-Up, Early Phase Operations, Phase II–III Monitoring, Clinical Safety, Quality & Compliance, Post-Marketing, Medical Devices (MDR), and M&A Support — across a multi-jurisdiction geography covering EU-27, UK, Turkey, Georgia, and accessible CIS markets.

What the process map reveals about AI readiness

The existing process documentation is detailed and structurally sound. Three honest observations are worth naming before moving to opportunities:

  1. Built from publicly available information The process map is built from publicly available information and operational logic. It correctly identifies the major workflows, but the actual friction points — where manual effort is highest, where errors accumulate, where time is lost — can only be validated by the people who run these processes daily. CRAs, Safety Processors, Regulatory Affairs leads, and Data Managers will have specific views that no external mapping can fully anticipate. Any innovation agenda must include structured interviews with these functional leads before committing to an automation roadmap.
  2. FSP changes the data situation The FSP model creates a structurally different data situation than the full-service CRO model. In FSP engagements, Biomapas staff work inside sponsor systems — meaning Biomapas has no proprietary data asset in that context at all. This limits the AI surfaces available for internal tooling and makes the innovation agenda more dependent on sponsor-level contract conversations than the full-service model.
  3. Operational depth is narrower than commercial footprint The geographic coverage stated commercially is broader than the operational revenue concentration. Based on public evidence and operational logic, the markets where Biomapas has genuine operational depth — established site networks, locally placed CRAs, active client portfolios — are Poland, France, Belgium, UK, Romania, Czech Republic, Slovakia, Bulgaria, the Baltics, the Netherlands, and Turkey. CIS markets (Kazakhstan, Ukraine, Armenia, Azerbaijan, Moldova) are strategically important and worth the investment in regulatory intelligence, but they are not yet at the scale that justifies primary technology investment. Any vendor selection, partnership model, or automation tool must be evaluated against the EU core + UK + Turkey geography first.
Part III · Application Map

Where automation and AI can create real value.

1
Compliance — do first

Compliance automation: non-negotiable, do it first.

These are the highest-priority automation opportunities. They operate on rules and structural data, carry no GDPR complexity, and the compliance risk of not building them is materially higher than any implementation risk. They are also the most credible things to demonstrate to pharma sponsors in bid defense — because they show that Biomapas operates with real-time quality infrastructure rather than retrospective spreadsheets.

SUSAR multi-jurisdiction routing engine

Suspected Unexpected Serious Adverse Reactions must be reported within 7 days (fatal/life-threatening) or 15 days (other) to multiple authorities depending on study geography — EMA EudraVigilance, MHRA, TUFAM, and individual member state competent authorities. Routing logic is a rule-based decision tree that follows directly from study country list, sponsor reporting model, and event characteristics. A validated routing engine reduces the largest single regulatory risk in the Clinical Safety service line and makes the compliance posture demonstrable to sponsors at bid defence.

Real-time eTMF completeness scoring

The Trial Master File must be inspection-ready continuously, not just before audits. Most CROs still rely on manual eTMF reviews at milestones. A real-time completeness scoring layer — running against the eTMF reference model and flagging missing or expiring documents per site, per visit, per regulatory milestone — is straightforward to build on top of Veeva Vault eTMF or equivalent and dramatically reduces audit findings. This is a near-zero-risk, near-zero-GDPR automation that pays back inside one trial.

Regulatory calendar engine across EU + UK + Turkey

The new EU CTR, UK CTR (effective 28 April 2026), Turkish TUFAM requirements, and country-specific substantial modification timelines create a multi-jurisdiction regulatory calendar that is currently maintained in spreadsheets and individual heads. A centralised engine that ingests protocol amendments, IB updates, DSUR cycles, and CTIS submission deadlines and outputs a per-trial, per-country regulatory action calendar — with assigned owners and escalation logic — converts a known operational pain point into a sponsor-visible service quality differentiator.

SAE / EDC reconciliation automation

Reconciliation between the safety database (Veeva Vault Safety / Argus) and the clinical EDC (Medidata Rave, Veeva Vault EDC, Castor) is a structural friction point in every trial: same event, two systems, divergent data fields, end-of-study reconciliation cycles that consume Data Manager and Safety Processor time. Vendor APIs now make automated reconciliation feasible — flagging discrepancies in real time rather than at database lock — and the residual human effort shifts to adjudication of genuine differences, not data hunting.

2
Productivity — invest deliberately

Productivity automation: real value, requires governance.

MedDRA auto-coding (clinical-side)

The same logic as in Pharmacovigilance applies to the clinical-side coding workload — adverse events captured in the EDC must be MedDRA-coded for analysis and CSR. AI-assisted coding with mandatory human confirmation is mature, vendor-supported in Medidata and Veeva, and saves substantial Data Manager time across a portfolio. Prerequisite is the same: locked active MedDRA version, systematic dictionary updates.

ICSR narrative auto-generation (clinical safety)

LLM-based first-draft narrative generation from structured EDC and safety database fields is the highest-yield language task in the Clinical Safety workflow. Constraint identical to PV: medically qualified human review and approval before submission. The workflow is AI-draft, human-edit, human-sign — not autonomous generation. Quality control on clinical accuracy must be instrumented from day one.

Monitoring Visit Report (MVR) auto-generation

This is one of the most concrete productivity opportunities in the entire vertical. CRA Monitoring Visit Reports are highly structured, follow a templated format, and pull from EDC data, query logs, drug accountability, and site visit notes. Vendor tooling (e.g. Veeva Vault CTMS visit-report agents, Medidata equivalents) and internal LLM workflows can produce a structured first-draft MVR in minutes from monitoring inputs — converting hours of CRA write-up time per visit into minutes of CRA review time. Across a Phase II–III portfolio of 8–15 active trials with multiple monitoring visits per site per quarter, this is a €120K–€180K annual capacity recovery, redirected to higher-value site management.

Protocol consistency checker (NLP)

Protocol amendments routinely introduce inconsistencies between the body of the protocol, the schedule of assessments, the inclusion/exclusion criteria, and the statistical analysis plan. An NLP consistency checker run before final issue catches these systematically — reducing protocol deviations downstream and demonstrating Clinical Strategy quality at bid defence. This is a relatively contained tool, buildable internally on top of an enterprise LLM API with the right prompt scaffolding and validation.

Enrolment prediction model (proprietary asset opportunity)

This is the most strategically important Tier 2 item — and the one most likely to become a proprietary Biomapas asset rather than a vendor licence. CEE site network performance data — recruitment rates by indication, by country, by site, screen-failure rates, dropout patterns — accumulated across ten-plus years of Biomapas trials, is genuinely proprietary. A predictive model built on top of this internal dataset, output as a sponsor-facing enrolment forecast at bid defence, is a differentiator that no global vendor can replicate without the underlying data. This is the single most defensible AI investment Biomapas can make in this vertical, and it is the kind of asset that increases acquisition value materially. Building it requires structured retrospective data extraction, statistical methodology design, and ongoing validation — a 12–18 month build, not a quick win, but worth the investment.

3
Differentiation — partner first

Strategic differentiation: partner before you build.

EHR-based pre-screening (sponsor-facing service line)

EHR-driven patient pre-screening — querying anonymised hospital electronic health record systems against protocol I/E criteria to identify candidate patients — is one of the highest-growth services in the global CRO market. Veeva, TriNetX, Deep6.ai, ConcertAI, and others operate in this layer. For Biomapas, this is a partnership play, not a build: the data sourcing, hospital agreements, and patient consent infrastructure required are out of scope for organic build. The right move is a partnership with one or two regional EHR/RWD platforms (e.g. TriNetX in EU, plus a CEE-specific partner), white-labelled into a Biomapas pre-screening service offer for sponsors running CEE-heavy trials. Margin is meaningful and the offer is differentiating in bid defence.

Digital twins / synthetic control arms

Unlearn.ai, Phesi, and Medidata's 3DS partnership are the leading vendors. Digital twin and synthetic control arm methods — using historical patient data to create probabilistic patient trajectories that augment or partially replace control arms — are accepted in regulatory dialogue (FDA and EMA both engaging) for specific therapeutic areas, principally rare disease, oncology, and neurology. This is a highly technical methodology layer; Biomapas's role is curation and partnership, not build. The strategic question is whether a digital-twin-enabled trial design service line is a credible 24–36 month offer for the rare disease and oncology client portfolio Biomapas already serves.

Real-World Evidence and PASS studies

Post-authorisation safety studies and real-world evidence generation are growing service lines on the boundary between Pharmacovigilance and Clinical Research. Biomapas's existing PV client base is a natural cross-sell origin for PASS work. Build-out requires a defined RWE methodology team, ethics committee experience for retrospective study designs, and partnership with one or more regional RWD platforms. This is a medium-term strategic build (24–48 months) anchored on existing client relationships rather than a near-term automation opportunity.

Part V · What Head of R&I Would Own

Owned scope, sequenced realistically.

The Head of Research & Innovation function inside the Clinical Research/Operations vertical does not displace the existing operational leadership. It sits adjacent to it, owning the capability roadmap, vendor relationships, and the AI-readiness of the operating layer — and intervening in service design only where AI changes the offer materially.

0–6 months — diagnostic and foundations

  1. Functional interviews and process validation. Structured interviews with Clinical Operations leadership, CRA leads, Safety Processors, Data Managers, Regulatory Affairs, and Quality. Validate the externally built process map against day-to-day reality. Identify the three highest-friction workflows the operations team would automate first if given the choice.
  2. Veeva Vault configuration audit. Confirm activation status of Vault AI agents per module, configuration completeness for AI prerequisites (controlled vocabularies, document classification, role-based workflows), and the gap between current configuration and the August 2026 Clinical Operations / Regulatory AI release.
  3. Data Processor compliance review. Inventory client master service agreements and Data Processing Agreements for AI training and secondary-use language. Identify the contracts that need amendment language and the contracts that effectively forbid internal AI use on client data today. This is the gate on every other AI initiative in this vertical.
  4. Compliance automation quick wins. Scope and initiate the four Tier 1 compliance automations (SUSAR routing, eTMF completeness, regulatory calendar, SAE/EDC reconciliation). Two of the four should be in pilot inside six months.

3–18 months — productivity and enrolment asset

  1. Productivity automation deployment. Roll out MedDRA auto-coding, ICSR narrative auto-generation, and MVR auto-generation across the active portfolio. Instrumentation matters: capture cycle-time and quality metrics from day one to support the next bid defence.
  2. Enrolment prediction model build. Begin the structured retrospective extraction of CEE site performance data, methodology design with a statistical partner, and first internal validation. Target a sponsor-facing v1 forecast tool inside 18 months.
  3. Vendor consolidation. Conclude the EHR / RWD partnership decision (TriNetX-anchored), conclude the digital twin evaluation (Unlearn vs Phesi vs neither), and formalise the Veeva Vault configuration roadmap through the December 2026 release.
  4. EU funding alignment. Position one or two of the AI initiatives — most credibly the enrolment prediction model and the SUSAR routing engine — into an EDCTP3, IHI, or Horizon Europe consortium application. The funding dimension is real; the work to access it is non-trivial and starts with consortium relationships, not with the application form.

12–36 months — service line redesign

  1. EHR pre-screening as a Biomapas-branded service. White-labelled offer for sponsors running CEE-heavy trials, anchored on TriNetX plus a regional EHR partner. This is the first new service line the function should produce.
  2. Digital twin / synthetic arm trial design service. Selective offer to the rare disease and oncology client base, partner-delivered. Decision gate at month 18 on whether the methodology investment is warranted.
  3. RWE / PASS service line build-out. Cross-sell into the existing Pharmacovigilance client base. Methodology team, ethics committee experience, RWD partner — a defined 24–36 month build.
  4. Acquisition narrative. By month 24, the AI capability story — proprietary enrolment model, configured Vault AI operating layer, compliance automation as a demonstrable competitive moat — must be articulated as part of Biomapas's enterprise value, not as a side note. This is what Carbyne Equity Partners is buying when the next valuation event comes.
Part VI · Workshop Questions

Five questions that decide whether this works.

The following are the questions that need honest answers in a leadership workshop before any of the above can be sequenced into a 2026 plan. They are not rhetorical and they do not have obvious answers.

  1. Are our Data Processing Agreements an enabling document or a blocking document? If most client contracts forbid secondary use of client data for AI training and tooling, then the proprietary enrolment model — and several Tier 2 productivity automations — cannot be built on client data without contract renegotiation. Honest answer: how many contracts would need amendment, and how willing are the largest five clients to engage on the conversation?
  2. What is the realistic Vault configuration maturity today? The August 2026 Veeva Clinical Operations / Regulatory AI release is the operational pivot point of this vertical. If Vault configuration is materially behind — controlled vocabularies, document classification, role-based workflows — then the AI release lands on infrastructure that cannot exploit it, and the calendar slips by a year. Honest answer: where is the configuration gap today, and what is the cost to close it before August?
  3. Where is the operational depth, really? The geographic coverage stated commercially is broader than the operational revenue concentration. Decisions on vendor selection, partnership models, and service line investment must be made against the EU-core-plus-UK-plus-Turkey reality, not against an aspirational map. Honest answer: which six countries account for most of the operational revenue today, and is that the right base to invest behind?
  4. Is the enrolment prediction model a strategic asset or a research project? The model becomes a defensible asset only if Biomapas commits to the structured data extraction, statistical methodology, and ongoing validation that turn site-network knowledge into a sponsor-facing forecasting tool. Otherwise it remains internal know-how that does not compound. Honest answer: is leadership prepared to commit a 12–18 month build with measurable milestones, or not?
  5. Where is FSP going as a share of revenue? In FSP engagements, Biomapas staff work inside sponsor systems and Biomapas owns no proprietary data asset in that context. If FSP grows as a share of total revenue, the AI investment thesis weakens because the data surface area for internal tooling shrinks. Honest answer: what is the planned FSP / full-service mix at €100M and €200M revenue, and does the AI roadmap match that mix?
Part VII · Revenue Connection

How this vertical contributes to the €200M target.

The €200M revenue target — roughly 9× the 2024 baseline — does not arrive from compliance automation. It arrives from three mechanisms that the Clinical Research/Operations vertical can credibly produce, all of which require the AI-readiness work above as a precondition.

  1. Higher win rate on bid defences. Sponsors increasingly include AI capability and operational instrumentation questions in the bid defence. A CRO that can demonstrate a real-time eTMF completeness dashboard, a multi-jurisdiction SUSAR routing engine, an MVR-Gen workflow with measured cycle-time savings, and a CEE-anchored enrolment prediction model wins more bids — particularly against AI-native boutique CROs in the mid-market segment. A 5–7 point improvement in bid win rate across the active sponsor portfolio is a multi-million-euro revenue effect, on existing client effort.
  2. New service lines anchored on existing client relationships. EHR pre-screening, digital-twin-enabled trial design, and RWE / PASS services are not net-new client acquisition. They are cross-sell into the existing Pharmacovigilance and Clinical Research client base, where the trust relationship is established and the contract vehicle exists. This is the highest-margin revenue growth available, and it is the part that compounds with each new client signed.
  3. Acquisition narrative and exit value. With Carbyne Equity Partners on the cap table since October 2024, the next valuation event is on a defined horizon. The proprietary enrolment prediction model, the configured Vault AI operating layer, the demonstrable compliance automation moat, and an EHR pre-screening service line under contract are the assets that move the multiple at exit. AI capability does not have to produce direct revenue inside Biomapas to produce enterprise value if it produces the right narrative for the strategic acquirer — which, in this segment, is one of the global CROs or a private equity roll-up vehicle.
Conclusion

Honest closing assessment.

The Clinical Research/Operations vertical is operationally complex, geographically distributed, and structurally exposed to a market that is moving from manual processes to AI-augmented workflows on a 12–24 month horizon. The work the Head of Research & Innovation function would own here is neither fashionable nor speculative: it is compliance automation that the regulators expect, productivity automation the sponsors will measure, and a small number of proprietary investments that turn ten years of CEE site network experience into an asset that compounds.

None of this is possible without two preconditions that sit outside this document: a structural answer to the Data Processor / Data Owner question through DPA renegotiation, and a Vault configuration maturity audit that closes the gap before the August 2026 AI release. Until those are in flight, every AI initiative in this vertical is gated on infrastructure that does not yet exist.

The bottom line for this vertical. Build compliance automation now because the regulators expect it. Build productivity automation deliberately because the sponsors will measure it. Build the enrolment prediction model because it is the only thing that becomes a proprietary asset. Partner on everything else, and stop trying to compete with vendors whose only product is a tool you can licence.
